I'm Jiaxin Zhang, a Ph.D. candidate in Electrical and Computer Engineering at
Johns Hopkins University, advised by
[Prof. Muyinatu Bell](https://pulselab.jhu.edu/muyinatu-a-lediju-bell-phd/). I
build medical imaging technologies at the intersection of **imaging physics,
signal processing, computational modeling, and AI**, with a particular focus on
medical ultrasound, photoacoustic imaging, and image-guided interventions.

Before Johns Hopkins, I was a research assistant at the
[W.M. Keck Center for Cellular Imaging](https://kcci.virginia.edu/), where I
worked on machine learning for cancer cell metabolism analysis in fluorescence
lifetime microscopy imaging. I received my M.Eng. in Electrical Engineering from
the University of Virginia, advised by
[Prof. Ammasi Periasamy](https://scholar.google.com/citations?user=Zr1uySwAAAAJ&hl=en)
and [Dr. Daniel Weller](https://scholar.google.com/citations?user=iDgXOJoAAAAJ&hl=en),
and my B.Eng. in Electrical Engineering from Nanjing University of Science and
Technology.

My strength is working across the **full imaging pipeline**, from raw signals,
acoustic physics, beamforming, and image reconstruction to differentiable
simulation, deep learning, 3D segmentation, and multimodal imaging. My background
in photoacoustics, FLIM, and optical instrumentation also gives me a broader
foundation in **biomedical optics and computational imaging**. I enjoy
understanding both how an image is formed and how computation and AI can make it
more useful.

My industry experience has helped me bring that perspective closer to real
products. At [Siemens Healthineers](https://www.siemens-healthineers.com/), I have
worked on differentiable 3D ultrasound simulation, synthetic data generation, and
AI-based cardiac image segmentation. At
[United Imaging Healthcare](https://usa.united-imaging.com/about/company), I
developed cardiac ultrasound image-processing algorithms using raw RF/IQ data and
worked on their integration and validation on clinical ultrasound systems. These
experiences taught me to think beyond algorithm performance and consider how a
method behaves with real data, real hardware, and real clinical workflows.

I'm **self-driven, fast-moving, and execution-focused**. I enjoy taking ownership
of open-ended problems, turning ideas into experiments quickly, and pushing
projects toward working solutions. I am also comfortable stepping outside my
existing expertise, which has taken me from biomedical optics to ultrasound, from
classical signal processing to deep learning, and from academic research to
industry R&D.

What excites me most is building **intelligent imaging systems that connect
physics, computation, and AI** to improve medical diagnosis, intervention, and
surgical guidance.
